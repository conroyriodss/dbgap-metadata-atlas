# Ten outstanding research questions

These are proposed workstreams, not preregistrations, completed analyses or assigned priorities. IDs remain stable for issues and references. The previous discussion suggested measurement validity and reuse provenance as foundations; the maintainer has not selected a final order.

## Q01. How much apparent activity reflects metadata coverage or accession structure?

**First analysis:** Audit high-, low- and discordant-activity roots; compare study-page literature with independent ascertainment, documented families and unique PMIDs.

**Readiness:** Saved records available; missing validation of bibliography completeness and biological families.

**Deliverable:** A ranking-stability and signal-completeness report, with explicit stale/failed-source sensitivity.

## Q02. What fraction of linked publications is verified secondary reuse?

**First analysis:** Blind, stratified human review of accession-paper evidence. Separate scientific use from relationship to the generating team, and retain indeterminate cases.

**Readiness:** Missing article-level evidence and independent labels; initial timing is not a reuse classifier.

**Deliverable:** Weighted reuse estimates, review agreement and influence distributions for the validated subset.

## Q03. Is the contributor/accessor community expanding or recurring?

**First analysis:** Resolve entities with review; cross-tabulate contributor-only, accessor-only and both. Separate own-family from other-family requests and annual first-observed/returning entities.

**Readiness:** Public labels and requester proxies available; missing identity validation and historical submission events.

**Deliverable:** A role-overlap and reach report with sensitivity to identity and family grouping.

## Q04. What characteristics distinguish all four DAR/publication profiles?

**First analysis:** Analyze both, DAR-only, publication-only and neither; examine size, phenotype depth, assay, consent, IC, study structure, age and metadata completeness.

**Readiness:** Exploratory current-data comparisons available; verified age and historical covariates incomplete.

**Deliverable:** Stratified profiles and, only if warranted, a parsimonious validated model using all four groups.

## Q05. Does DAR information predict later scientific output beyond baseline metadata?

**First analysis:** Compare baseline models to models adding requester breadth, repeat activity and stated purpose. Use fixed temporal landmarks, later test cohorts and grouped-family validation.

**Readiness:** Needs baseline histories and independently validated reuse outcomes; operational conversion requires additional logs.

**Deliverable:** Calibration and incremental prediction performance with no future-information leakage.

## Q06. Do richer data and substantive updates attract new users or questions?

**First analysis:** Link dated additions of participants, phenotypes or assays to subsequent requester and verified-publication change; compare appropriate nonupdated resources.

**Readiness:** Current composition available; historical update contents/dates incomplete. Demand can motivate updates.

**Deliverable:** An update-life-cycle study separating activity volume from extension to new research domains.

## Q07. Can datasets without early requests develop later demand?

**First analysis:** Estimate first request in years 3-6 conditional on no request through year 3; censor correctly. Analyze first demand separately from later inactivity.

**Readiness:** Needs validated availability dates and sufficiently complete histories; storage implications also need reads, bytes, restore costs and delays.

**Deliverable:** Finite-horizon probabilities and risk sets, not an unsupported never-used classification.

## Q08. What explains BDC publication/DAR differences?

**First analysis:** Decompose selection and ascertainment within program, age, modality, size, consent and family strata. Add first-listing dates and platform-native logs for longitudinal work.

**Readiness:** Composition analysis possible now; exposure timing and operational measurements needed for effects.

**Deliverable:** A selection/coverage analysis; causal claims remain out of scope without an adequate design.

## Q09. What roles do dataset-trial references actually represent?

**First analysis:** Audit every recorded pair using exact page/PMID evidence; classify originating, ancillary, related-literature, downstream, incorrect and unresolved relationships.

**Readiness:** Small bounded reference set available; semantic agreement does not supply relationship provenance.

**Deliverable:** A complete documented clinical-linkage reference set and separate semantic-matching validation.

## Q10. What NIH support is attributable to generation, reuse or infrastructure?

**First analysis:** Compare explicit study acknowledgements, paper acknowledgements and RePORTER; review award descriptions, periods and publication provenance for role attribution.

**Readiness:** Many-to-many links available; funding roles and attributable cost are not established.

**Deliverable:** A bounded support-role network with uncertainty, not full-award cost-per-paper or ROI.
